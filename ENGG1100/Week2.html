Project Limitations & Requirements

Dimensional Constraints

  • Area: The minimum total area is 48000 mm².

  • Footprint: The maximum length is 300mm, and the maximum width is 250mm.

  • Overall Height: The total height, including the mounted locator tag, must be 280mm.

  • Base Height: The vertical distance from the base to the floor (door height) cannot exceed 80mm.

  • Containment: Both the structure and drive subsystems must fit entirely within these dimensional limits before deployment.

Structural & Functional Requirements

  • Interior Space: The center of the building must have an open gathering space measuring at least 155mm x 155mm x 150mm.

  • Openings: You must include representative doors and windows that can be opened, with minimum dimensions of 50mm x 30mm.

  • Baseplate Attachment: No rigid attachments to the baseplate can be longer than 40mm, though cables do not count as rigid attachments.

  • Buoyant Contact: While floating, the system is allowed a maximum of 2 points of contact with the baseplate, and each contact point cannot exceed an area of 20mm x 20mm.

Power & Control Limitations

  • Power Supply: Power is limited to a maximum of 12V at 2A.

  • Battery Safety: The use of lithium batteries is strictly prohibited.

  • Control Systems: The prototype can be controlled manually, or via fully/semi-autonomous wired or wireless remotes.

  • Cabling: External cables and remote components are exempt from the strict size limits, but any tethering must not interfere with the structure's movement during the test.

Budget Constraints

  • Maximum Cap: The final prototype used during the demonstration cannot cost more than $250.

  • Scoring Note: While $250 is the hard cap, achieving the maximum score for the budget section requires keeping the total cost at or below $170.

Waterproofing Method

Physical Isolation of Core Electronics

  • Food-Grade Airtight Containers (Tupperware/Sistema): Utilizing a small plastic container with a silicone gasket is a high-performance, low-cost solution. The transparent material allows for real-time monitoring of internal status LEDs.

  • Custom 3D Printed Enclosures: Note that FDM prints are prone to capillary leakage due to layer lines. Mitigation: Coat the interior with epoxy resin or thick acrylic varnish. Seal the lid interface with silicone sealant or a custom-printed TPU flexible gasket.

Cable Entries

  • Cable Glands (PG Joints): The industry standard. By drilling a hole and installing a gland, the internal rubber ring compresses around the wire as the nut is tightened, creating an absolute seal. Available at Jaycar.

  • Hot Glue: Do not rely solely on hot glue. It becomes brittle in cold water and peels easily from smooth plastic.

  • Silicone: If cable glands are not used, use bathroom-grade silicone sealant. Its superior adhesion and elasticity allow it to maintain a seal even when cables are tugged.

Drive System & Shaft Sealing

  • Stuffing Tubes (Stern Tubes): A standard RC boat technique. Insert a brass tube through the hull, fill it with marine (or Vaseline), and pass the propeller shaft through. The grease acts as a barrier against water pressure while allowing high-speed rotation.

  • Direct Submersion (The "Quick & Dirty" Method): Standard brushed DC motors can actually operate underwater temporarily (water acts as an insulator and coolant), though friction and oxidation will eventually limit their lifespan. For a short prototype test, you can submerge cheap motors directly, provided the solder joints are fully encapsulated in epoxy to prevent short circuits.

  • Magnetic Coupling: A high-end solution where an internal magnet (on the motor) drives an external magnet (on the propeller) through the plastic hull. This ensures 100% isolation but increases design complexity and cost.

Comparison: Specialized Waterproof Motors vs. Direct Submersion

Verdict: Given the project constraints and the $170 budget score limit, using Standard Brushed Motors is the more strategic choice.

PCB-Level Secondary Defence

  • Conformal Coating: A transparent insulating film applied to the ESP32 and driver boards (avoiding pins and heatsinks). It protects against condensation or minor splashes. Clear nail polish serves as an effective, budget-friendly alternative.

  • Adhesive-Lined Heat Shrink: When soldering external motor or sensor wires, standard heat shrink is insufficient. Use marine-grade (dual-wall) heat shrink; the internal adhesive melts during heating to create a hermetic seal around the joint.

Design and Implementation of Power and Control Systems

Sensor Integration for Fully-autonomous Control System

1. Stability Control (30% of Total Score)

The stability score is calculated as 30×[25−S20], where S is the absolute integral of tilttime. Minimizing tilt during the 2kg weight shift and 2m/s wind test is critical.

  • Recommended Sensor: MPU6050 (6-Axis IMU)

  • Engineering Application: Provides real-time Pitch and Roll data. The microcontroller (MCU) uses this feedback to trigger differential thrust from the motors, actively "levelling" the house against asymmetric loads or wind gusts.

2. Station Keeping (10% of Total Score)

This score is determined by the final displacement from the origin in the 826mm×583mm test tank after the water recedes.

  • Option A: VL53L0X (ToF Laser Ranging)

    Mounted on X and Y axes to measure the absolute distance to the tank's aluminium profile edges. Its narrow beam avoids interference from water ripples.

  • Option B: Rotary Encoder + Tether (Mechanical Solution)

    Exploits the rule that "cables are not considered rigid connections." Using thin nylon lines connected to the 380mm×380mm contact plate, the encoder tracks precise displacement pulses as the house drifts, unaffected by waves or tilt.

3. Power Efficiency (10% of Total Score)

Efficiency is scored as 10×[100−(P−10)100], where P is total mAh consumed. The system is limited to a 12V 2A supply.

  • Recommended Sensor: INA219 (DC Current/Voltage/Power Monitor)

  • Engineering Application: Monitors real-time mA draw. It allows the MCU to implement PWM limiting to prevent exceeding the 2A threshold (which triggers a power cut-off) and helps identify the most energy-efficient propeller/motor configurations during prototyping.

4. Automated Energy Saving: Water Level Detection

Water levels fluctuate between 100mm and 200mm during the test.

  • Recommended Sensor: Water Contact Probe or Float Switch

  • Application: A "strategic" tool for maximizing efficiency. Once the water level drops below 100mm and the house settles on the contact plate, the sensor triggers a forced shutdown of all motors. This prevents wasting power (P) when propulsion is no longer required, ensuring a higher efficiency score.

Suitable Microcontroller Comparison: ESP32 vs. Arduino (Uno/Nano)

Verdict: Using ESP32 is the more strategic choice due to its Maximum space efficiency, superior processing ability and modern architecture.

Suggested Power Architecture

The test bench provides a 12V 2A source, which exceeds the direct input voltage limit of the ESP32. Therefore, a cost-effective Buck Converter (e.g., LM2596 or MP1584) is essential to step down the 12V to a stable 5V or 3.3V before powering the microcontroller.

Development Roadmap for Autonomous Control System

Phase 1: Pure Manual Mode – Hardware Validation & Calibration

In this stage, the ESP32 acts as a wireless receiver, while the "brain" is the human operator.

  • Implementation: Build a lightweight control panel using WebSockets to send PWM commands to the motors via sliders or a virtual joystick.

  • Core Objectives:

    • System Stress Test: Verify if the Buck Converter overheats, check for leaks, and ensure total current stays below the 2A bench limit.

    • Physical Calibration: Record the exact PWM values needed to counteract the 2m/s wind and the 2kg weight shift. These benchmarks are essential for your future PID tuning.

  • Scoring Impact: Secures basic structural and functional points, but manual reaction time is too slow to minimize the "tilt-over-time" penalty.

Phase 2: Semi-Automatic Mode – Attitude Stabilization (The "Sweet Spot")

This introduces "Attitude Mode," similar to drone flight controllers. It is the most cost-effective stage for high scores.

  • Implementation: The MPU6050 is integrated. The ESP32 runs a PID closed-loop algorithm with one goal: locking the Roll and Pitch at 0 degrees.

  • Engineering Logic:

    • Auto-Leveling: If the 2kg weight moves, the ESP32 detects the tilt in milliseconds and automatically increases motor thrust to level the house.

    • Manual Positioning: You only use the remote to "drive" the house back to the center of the tank if it drifts.

  • Scoring Impact: High. By automating stability, you minimize the tilt integral (30 pts). Manual positioning is sufficient for the Station Keeping (10 pts) and avoids the complexity of full automation.

Phase 3: Fully Autonomous Mode – The Ultimate Closed-Loop System

This version aims for a perfect score.

  • Implementation: Remove the remote control. The system uses "Cascade PID" control.

    • Outer Loop (Position): Uses ToF sensors or encoders to detect drift and calculate the necessary return thrust.

    • Inner Loop (Attitude): Combines thrust requirements with real-time leveling.

  • Engineering Logic: Requires robust Fail-safe logic. If the position sensor data becomes noisy due to water ripples, the system must automatically downgrade to "Semi-Automatic" to prevent erratic movement.

  • Scoring Impact: Potential for a perfect score, but highly risky due to sensor interference and complex tuning.

Propulsion and Anchoring Strategies

Propulsion Systems (Active Counter-Wind Thrust)

These traditional power solutions use water or air displacement to counteract wind drift.

Option 1: Air Propeller Propulsion (Airboat Style)

  • Concept: Install micro DC motors with drone propellers on the roof or high sidewalls to generate forward thrust by blowing air backward.

  • Advantages: The motors and wiring remain entirely above the waterline, completely eliminating complex waterproofing issues.

  • Disadvantages: The high thrust point creates a leverage moment when the wind blows, increasing the risk of capsizing. This requires extremely high precision from the PID stability algorithm.

Option 2: Underwater Thruster Propulsion

  • Concept: Deploy motors and marine propellers with waterproof shaft sleeves below the waterline.

  • Advantages: Underwater propulsion is highly efficient, and since the thrust point is at the bottom (near the Center of Gravity), it provides excellent stability.

  • Disadvantages: The waterproofing process is technically demanding. As previously discussed, commercial waterproof thrusters are expensive, and DIY seals are prone to leakage.

Option 3: Water Jet Propulsion

  • Concept: Submerge inexpensive submersible pumps (or aquarium pumps) within the base. Water is drawn in and expelled through nozzles via silicone tubing to generate reactive force.

  • Advantages: Submersible pumps are natively waterproof; the lack of exposed rotating blades ensures high safety.

  • Disadvantages: Pumps are relatively heavy, and the response time for water jetting is slower than direct propeller rotation.

Anchoring Solutions (Physical Displacement Restriction)

These strategies involve physical connections to secure the house. Note the strict constraints: rigid connections to the baseplate cannot exceed 40mm, and there are a maximum of two contact points (20mm×20mm each). Since flood levels reach 200mm, a 40mm rigid rod will not reach the floor.

Option 4: Smart Winch & Flexible Tethering

  • Concept: This exploits a "loophole" in the guidelines—cables are not considered rigid connections. You can attach two nylon fishing lines to the center of the baseplate, with the other ends connected to motorized "winches" inside the house. When the house drifts, the motors reel in the line to pull the house back to the origin.

  • Advantages: Perfectly follows the rules, remains unaffected by wind intensity, and consumes very low power.

  • Disadvantages: The mechanical design for winding and preventing tangles is complex. Additionally, cables must not interfere with structural movement, requiring constant tension management.

Option 5: Drag Anchor / Sea Anchor (Weighted Friction)

  • Concept: Hang a precisely calculated metal weight from the bottom of the house so that it drags along the floor of the test bench. While buoyancy lifts the house, the friction from the weight acts as an anchor to slow down drifting.

  • Advantages: Passive mechanical design requiring zero motors and $0 cost.

  • Disadvantages: This only "slows" drift rather than "actively returning" to the origin. Excessive friction may cause the house to tilt or even sink in deeper water.
